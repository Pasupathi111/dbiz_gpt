SECTION 1: EMPLOYMENT TYPES
================================================================================

1.1 Purpose and Scope
This section defines the five categories of engagement recognised by DBizLabs
and sets out the rights, entitlements, and obligations applicable to each.
Classification is determined at the time of offer by the hiring manager in
consultation with People Operations and is reflected in the individual's
letter of employment or service contract.

1.2 Full-Time Employment (Permanent)
A Full-Time Permanent Employee is one who is engaged on an indefinite-term
employment contract to work the standard working hours specified in Section 3
for the Company. This is the default employment type for all roles from Grade
A through Grade E+ and carries the full suite of entitlements described in
this Handbook.

Rights and entitlements include:
  (a) All leave entitlements in Section 4 (Annual, Sick, Maternity, etc.)
  (b) Full compensation and benefits package under Section 5
  (c) Eligibility for AWS, bonuses, and ESOPs (per Section 5)
  (d) Participation in performance management cycle (Section 6)
  (e) CPF contributions (Singapore citizens and PRs only)
  (f) Access to company L&D funds, wellness programmes, and insurance

1.3 Part-Time Employment
A Part-Time Employee is one who works fewer than 35 hours per week on a
regular and recurring basis, typically under a formal part-time employment
contract. This arrangement is most common in administrative support,
customer success, and certain training roles.

Part-Time employees are entitled to:
  (a) Annual leave, sick leave, and other leave types pro-rated to actual
      contracted hours vs. a standard 44-hour week
  (b) Access to medical and dental benefits on a pro-rated basis
  (c) CPF contributions where applicable (minimum S$50/month salary threshold)
  (d) AWS on a pro-rated basis if employed for the full calendar year
  (e) Performance management participation (simplified half-year cycle)

Part-Time employees are NOT eligible for:
  - ESOP option grants (unless specifically approved by the CEO)
  - Housing loan benefit under Section 5.14
  - Full L&D lifetime fund (pro-rated S$5,000 lifetime cap)

1.4 Contract Employment (Fixed-Term)
A Contract Employee is one who is engaged under a fixed-term contract for a
specified period (minimum 3 months, maximum 24 months) and a defined scope of
work. Fixed-term contracts are typically used for:
  - Project-based roles with a clearly defined deliverable and end date
  - Maternity or long-service leave coverage
  - Specialist skills required for a limited engagement period

Contract Employees are entitled to:
  (a) Annual leave pro-rated at a rate of 7% of gross hourly rate (or as
      specified in the employment contract)
  (b) Sick leave pro-rated (minimum 5 days for contracts >= 6 months)
  (c) CPF contributions (Singapore citizens/PRs where statutory)
  (d) Access to office facilities, equipment, and training necessary for role
  (e) Workers' compensation insurance as required by law

Contract Employees are NOT eligible for:
  - AWS, performance bonuses, or ESOP grants
  - Medical, dental, or wellness reimbursements
  - Housing loan or study leave benefits
  - Notice of non-renewal (contract expires automatically on end date)

Note: Contract Employees who have completed 24 months of continuous contract
service may be considered for conversion to full-time permanent status at the
discretion of the hiring manager and People Operations. Conversion rates over
the past 24 months have averaged 38%.

1.5 Internship Programme
An Intern is a student or recent graduate engaged on a short-term basis
(typically 10 to 24 weeks) primarily for training and development purposes.
DBizLabs runs two structured internship cohorts annually:
  - Summer Internship: May–August (applications open February)
  - Winter Internship: November–February (applications open August)

Intern entitlements and working conditions:
  (a) Monthly internship allowance:
      - Undergraduate interns (Year 1–3): S$1,800 per month
      - Final-year undergraduates / Masters students: S$2,400 per month
      - PhD candidates (research internships): S$3,200 per month
  (b) Working hours: 9:00–18:00 Monday to Friday (strictly no overtime)
  (c) Leave: 1 day of personal leave per month of internship (pro-rated)
  (d) Equipment: Company laptop (MacBook Air 13" or equivalent) for duration
  (e) Access: All internal systems (email, Slack, Jira, Confluence) with
      role-based access controls; no access to production environments
  (f) Insurance: Work-injury coverage only; no group medical insurance
  (g) Mentorship: Assigned full-time technical/business mentor + 1:1 with
      People Operations every 2 weeks
  (h) Stipend: S$20 per day meal and transport allowance (in addition to above)

All interns are required to present a final project showcase to their team
and People Operations on the last day of their internship. Outstanding interns
may receive a return offer for full-time employment or a subsequent internship.

1.6 Temporary Employment
A Temporary Employee is one who is engaged on a short-term ad-hoc basis via
an approved third-party staffing agency. This employment type is strictly
limited to:
  - Covering short absences (< 3 months) such as short sick leave or
    unexpected departures while permanent recruitment is in progress
  - Event-based roles such as conference staffing, DBiz Academy training
    assistants, or campus recruitment drives
  - One-off administrative, data entry, or research support projects

Temporary employees are not DBizLabs employees; they remain employees of the
relevant staffing agency and are subject to that agency's terms and conditions
of employment. DBizLabs has no employment relationship, liability, or
obligation beyond the contractual arrangement with the staffing firm.

Managers wishing to engage temporary staff must submit a Form STA-01 to
Finance and People Operations at least 10 business days in advance. No
temporary staff may be engaged via verbal arrangements or without an approved
purchase order in place. Rates payable to staffing agencies are capped by
the rate card published in the Finance Portal.

1.7 Employment Type Transitions
Requests to change employment type (e.g. from Part-Time to Full-Time, or
Contract to Permanent) must be initiated by the line manager via Form ET-02
submitted to People Operations. All transitions require:
  - Written justification from the line manager
  - Endorsement from the relevant department head (D-grade or above)
  - Approval from People Operations and the CFO (for permanent headcount budget)
  - A minimum of 6 months of satisfactory performance in the current role

Transitions are effective on the first day of the calendar month following
approval unless otherwise agreed. Leave balances, benefits, and compensation
are adjusted from the transition date; there is no retro-active adjustment.

1.8 Correct Classification
It is the responsibility of both managers and employees to ensure that the
employment classification reflects the actual nature of the working
arrangement. Any employee who believes that their classification is
incorrect or does not match their role should raise the matter with
People Operations for a confidential review. DBizLabs is committed to full
compliance with the Singapore Employment Act and all applicable regulations
in every jurisdiction where we operate. Misclassification audits are
conducted semi-annually by Internal Audit and the external auditors.

1.9 Employment Act Coverage
All Full-Time, Part-Time, and Contract employees earning a monthly salary
of S$4,500 or below are covered under Part IV of the Singapore Employment
Act. Employees earning above S$4,500 per month are not covered under Part IV
(Rest Days, Hours of Work, Overtime) but remain covered under all other
parts of the Act. This Handbook provides terms that are, in all cases, at
least as favourable as the statutory minimums required under the Employment
Act and any other applicable legislation.

1.10 Records of Employment
DBizLabs maintains an official employee file (both physical and electronic)
for each employee for the duration of employment and for a minimum of 7 years
following cessation. Employees may request access to their own personnel file
by submitting Form PR-07 to People Operations. Files may be inspected in the
presence of a People Operations staff member; copies of documents may be
requested subject to applicable fees and data privacy regulations under the
Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA).

SECTION 2: ONBOARDING & PROBATION
================================================================================

2.1 Pre-Employment Requirements
Before your official start date at DBizLabs, you will be required to complete
a number of pre-employment checks and provide the following documentation
to People Operations. Failure to complete these requirements may result in
a delayed or cancelled start date.

Pre-employment checklist:
  (a) Signed and returned Letter of Offer or Contract of Service
  (b) Signed Confidentiality, Non-Disclosure and IP Assignment Agreement
  (c) Singapore NRIC (front and back) or Passport + Employment Pass / S Pass /
      Work Permit (for foreigners)
  (d) Educational certificates (highest qualification + professional certs)
  (e) Proof of past employment (last 2 years of CPF statements, or payslips)
  (f) Latest passport-sized photograph (digital copy, minimum 400x514px)
  (g) Completed Medical Declaration Form (MED-01)
  (h) Results of pre-employment medical check-up (for roles Grade C and above,
      or roles with access to customer production data or financial systems)
  (i) Emergency contact details (name, relationship, mobile number, email)
  (j) Bank account details for payroll disbursement (Singapore DBS/POSB/OCBC/UOB
      preferred; other banks require Finance approval)

Criminal declaration: All candidates are required to declare any prior criminal
convictions, whether spent or unspent under the Registration of Criminals Act.
Failure to declare a material conviction may be grounds for immediate dismissal
without notice. DBizLabs conducts pre-employment screening via a licensed
vendor for all roles at Grade C+ and above, and for all roles in Finance,
Legal, Internal Audit, and Customer Engineering with access to production data.

2.2 Day One Onboarding Experience
On your official start date, please report to the Level 22 Reception Desk at
Marina One West Tower no later than 09:45. The Day One programme runs from
09:45 to approximately 17:30 and includes:

Morning Session (09:45 – 12:00):
  - Welcome coffee and introduction with People Operations team
  - Completion of any remaining paperwork (tax forms, CPF nomination, etc.)
  - Collection of company equipment (laptop, monitor, accessories, ID badge)
  - IT account setup: email, Slack, Jira, Confluence, 1Password, Okta SSO
  - Office tour: workstations, meeting rooms, nursing room, prayer room,
    cafeteria, pantry, first-aid station, emergency exits

Lunch (12:00 – 13:30):
  - Team welcome lunch at a nearby restaurant (expensed by the hiring manager)
  - No official business is conducted during lunch; please enjoy the meal

Afternoon Session (13:30 – 17:30):
  - Company orientation: Mission, values, strategy, FY2026 plan (by HRBP)
  - Overview of this Employee Handbook Version 4.2
  - Introduction to the Performance Management framework (Section 6)
  - Compliance and code of conduct training (Section 7)
  - Data privacy and information security training
  - 30-minute 1:1 with your direct manager to discuss first-week priorities
  - Meet-and-greet with your immediate team members and cross-functional peers

By end of Day One you should have:
  (a) Working laptop and all required systems access
  (b) Assigned desk or hot-desk booking access
  (c) A clear list of tasks and objectives for your first week
  (d) Your onboarding buddy's contact details

2.3 The First 30 Days (Buddy System)
Every new joiner is assigned an Onboarding Buddy during the first week. The
Buddy is typically a peer in the same or adjacent team who has been at
DBizLabs for at least 12 months and volunteered for the role. Your Buddy's
responsibilities include:

  - Checking in with you informally at least twice per week for the first month
  - Explaining team-specific processes, norms, and "unwritten rules"
  - Introducing you to key stakeholders inside and outside the team
  - Helping you navigate the office, understand tooling, and find documentation
  - Being a safe sounding board for questions you may not want to ask your manager

If after two weeks you have not been assigned a Buddy, or if the Buddy
arrangement is not working well, please contact your HR Business Partner
(HRBP) who will arrange an alternative.

2.4 Probation Period Overview
All new employees (Full-Time and Part-Time permanent) serve a probation
period at the beginning of their employment. The purpose of probation is to:
  - Give the employee a fair opportunity to learn the role and integrate into
    the team and company culture
  - Allow the manager to assess the employee's fit against role expectations
  - Provide a structured feedback mechanism with clear goals and check-ins
  - Establish a foundation for long-term success within the organisation

Probation periods vary by employee grade:
  - Grade A:  3 calendar months from start date
  - Grade B:  3 calendar months from start date
  - Grade C:  3 calendar months from start date
  - Grade C+: 6 calendar months from start date
  - Grade D:  6 calendar months from start date
  - Grade E:  6 calendar months from start date
  - Grade E+: 6 calendar months from start date (and CEO approval required
              for confirmation)

For Part-Time employees, the probation period is extended proportionally to
reflect the reduced number of working hours. For example, a Grade B employee
working 3 days per week (60%) will serve a 5-month probation period.

Interns and temporary employees do not serve probation; the engagement period
is itself treated as an evaluation window. Contract employees serving
contracts of 12 months or longer serve a 3-month probationary period at the
beginning of the contract, regardless of grade.

2.5 Probation Goals and Check-Ins
Within the first 5 business days of joining, your manager must create a
Probation Plan document in Lattice (our HR platform) containing:
  (a) 3–5 specific, measurable goals aligned to the role description
  (b) Clear success criteria for each goal (what "good" looks like)
  (c) Key stakeholders to collaborate with
  (d) Any required training, certifications, or shadowing

Mandatory formal check-ins during probation:
  - End of Week 2: Informal 30-minute touch base (manager + employee)
  - Mid-probation (50% of period elapsed): Formal 60-minute documented
    review using Form PB-03. Must include written progress notes against
    each goal and an overall rating: On Track / Needs Improvement / At Risk
  - Final review (2 weeks before probation end): Formal 90-minute discussion
    with a clear recommendation: Confirm / Extend / Terminate

2.6 Confirmation, Extension, or Termination
No later than 5 working days before the scheduled end of the probation
period, the line manager must submit Form PB-05 to People Operations
containing one of three recommendations:

Option 1: CONFIRM in role (Passed Probation)
  - This is the default outcome for the majority of employees.
  - When confirmed, the employee moves to permanent status and all
    entitlements in this Handbook apply retroactively from the start date
    (except ESOP cliff, which follows the standard ESOP agreement).
  - A written Confirmation Letter is issued by People Operations within
    5 working days.

Option 2: EXTEND probation period
  - Probation may be extended by up to one additional period of the same
    length (3 or 6 months). Extensions are permitted only once per employee
    and require written justification.
  - Common grounds for extension: extended leave taken during probation,
    significant project delays that prevented fair evaluation, or an
    "At Risk" mid-point review followed by genuine improvement.
  - A written Extension Letter is required, signed by both the manager and
    HRBP, with clear milestones for the extension period.

Option 3: TERMINATE employment during or at end of probation
  - Termination may occur if, despite adequate support and feedback, the
    employee is unable to meet the minimum requirements of the role.
  - During probation, the notice period is 14 calendar days (or payment
    in lieu thereof) from either party. See Appendix B for full details.
  - All termination decisions during probation must be reviewed and approved
    by both People Operations and Legal before being communicated.

2.7 Internal Transfers and Probation
If an employee transfers to a new role or department internally within
DBizLabs, they may be required to serve a secondary "probation" (typically
3 months) in the new role. This does not affect permanent status, salary,
or benefits, but provides a structured evaluation period for the new role.
Unsuccessful completion of an internal probation will result in either a
return to the previous role (if available) or the beginning of a 30-day
outplacement support period.

2.8 Offboarding for Non-Confirmed Employees
Employees who do not pass probation are entitled to:
  - Salary and applicable allowances up to the last working day
  - Pro-rated annual leave (if any accrued but untaken) paid as salary
  - CPF contributions up to the last working day
  - Return of all personal property submitted pre-employment
  - Option to collect a Certificate of Service upon request

Non-confirmed employees are not eligible for AWS, bonuses, or pro-rated
ESOP vesting. Access to all systems is revoked on the last working day.

2.9 Continuous Employment
For the avoidance of doubt, the probation period counts towards continuous
employment for all purposes including leave calculation, severance pay,
ESOP cliff vesting, and the 2-year qualifying period for the Housing Loan
benefit in Section 5.14.

SECTION 3: WORKING HOURS, ATTENDANCE & FLEXIBILITY
================================================================================

3.1 Standard Core Working Hours
The official working week for DBizLabs Singapore Headquarters office is:

  MONDAY to FRIDAY:  09:00 to 18:00
  LUNCH BREAK:       1 hour (any time between 12:00 and 14:00)

This represents a standard 40-hour working week excluding the lunch break.
Teams operating in other regions (Japan, India, UK, UAE, US, Germany) follow
local working hours as communicated by the country manager and HRBP. These
regional standards will always be at least as favourable as local statutory
requirements (e.g. the UK Working Time Regulations 1998).

3.2 Core Hours and Flexi-Time (Flexible Working Hours Policy)
DBizLabs operates a flexi-time policy to accommodate individual preferences,
caregiving responsibilities, and commuting arrangements. Flexi-time works as
follows:

CORE HOURS (when physical or virtual presence is mandatory for meetings,
collaboration, and ceremonies):
  10:00 – 12:00 (morning window)
  14:30 – 16:30 (afternoon window)

Flexible arrival window: Employee may start any time between 08:00 and 10:00.
Flexible departure window: Employee may finish any time between 17:00 and 19:00.

However, a deviation of more than +/- 1 hour from the standard 09:00 start
requires prior written approval from the line manager via Form FL-01.

Examples:
  (a) Employee wishes to come in at 08:00 and leave at 17:00. This is within
      the +/- 1 hour band — no approval required. The employee simply ensures
      their calendar shows "Out of Office: Flexible Hours" before 09:00.
  (b) Employee wishes to come in at 11:00 and leave at 20:00. This is +2 hours
      from the standard start. Requires line manager approval via Form FL-01
      submitted minimum 2 days in advance.

Approval should be granted where the arrangement does not impair teamwork,
client meetings, or core business operations. Managers are encouraged to
approve reasonable flexi-time requests and to model work-life balance.

3.3 Remote and Hybrid Work Policy
Effective 1 August 2026, DBizLabs operates a hybrid working model. All
employees are permitted to work remotely from their home address on file
for up to 2 days per week. This is commonly known as "2D/3W" (2 days from
home, 3 days in the office).

Key rules:
  (a) 2 remote days per week is the default maximum. Employees are required
      to be present in the office on at least 3 days per calendar week
      (Monday–Friday).
  (b) The 3 office days are determined by team agreement and published in
      advance. Most teams have a fixed pattern (e.g. Tue/Wed/Thu in office).
  (c) Individual employees may vary which specific days they work remotely
      from week to week, provided the 3-day minimum office presence is met
      and team meetings are attended.
  (d) Remote days must be logged in the Desk Booking System (DBizBook) by
      23:59 the previous Sunday. This is to ensure correct office capacity
      planning for meeting rooms, desks, and catering.
  (e) During remote days, employees must:
      - Be reachable on Slack and phone within 15 minutes during core hours
      - Maintain a suitable, safe work area (see Section 8 on Home Safety)
      - Use the company VPN when accessing internal systems or customer data
      - Have camera switched on for all meetings of 3 or more people
      - Not engage in unrelated full-time or part-time employment

Exceptions requiring HRBP approval (Form FL-03):
  - Temporary increased remote work (up to 4 days/week) for a max 4 weeks
    per year for medical, caregiving, or relocation reasons
  - Fully remote roles (less than 1 day per month in the office) — these are
    limited to specific job families (e.g. some specialist engineering roles
    in regions outside Singapore) and require CFO + COO sign-off. As of the
    publication of this Handbook, 14 people hold fully remote agreements.

3.4 Attendance Tracking and Tardiness
DBizLabs operates a trust-based model for attendance; there is no physical
punch-card system. However, accurate records of attendance are maintained
through:
  - Desk booking system (DBizBook) records in-office vs. remote days
  - Calendar data (for core-hours meeting attendance)
  - Manager-led spot checks and observation

Employees who are going to be late arriving (either physically or virtually)
must notify their line manager via Slack SMS no later than 09:15 on the day.
Repeated tardiness (defined as 3+ occurrences in any 30-day window, each
being 15 minutes or more beyond agreed start time) will be addressed through
the informal feedback channel and may be escalated to formal counselling or
a performance improvement plan if not corrected.

3.5 Overtime and Compensatory Time Off (CTO)
For employees covered under Part IV of the Singapore Employment Act
(monthly salary <= S$4,500, typically Grade A and entry-level Grade B):
  - Overtime work is authorised at time-and-a-half (1.5x) of the hourly
    basic rate of pay.
  - The maximum permissible overtime is 72 hours per month.
  - Overtime must be pre-approved by the line manager via Form OT-01.
  - Unpaid overtime is not permitted for EA-covered employees.

For employees NOT covered under Part IV (all Grade C+ and above, and most
Grade B employees earning above S$4,500/month):
  - Overtime compensation is provided via Compensatory Time Off (CTO) at a
    rate of 1 hour CTO per 1 hour of approved overtime, rounded up to the
    nearest half hour.
  - CTO must be approved by the manager in advance (exceptions apply for
    genuine emergency out-of-hours work such as incident response).
  - CTO must be taken within 90 days of being accrued; any CTO not taken
    within 90 days is forfeited.
  - There is no cash payment in lieu of CTO for non-EA employees except in
    the case of employment termination, when all accrued CTO (up to a max
    of 40 hours) is paid out at basic salary.

3.6 Public Holidays
DBizLabs observes all gazetted public holidays in Singapore as published by
the Ministry of Manpower each year. For FY2026 the following 11 public
holidays are observed:

  1. New Year's Day       – Thu 1 Jan 2026
  2. Chinese New Year     – Tue 17 Feb 2026
  3. Chinese New Year     – Wed 18 Feb 2026
  4. Good Friday          – Fri 3 Apr 2026
  5. Labour Day           – Fri 1 May 2026
  6. Vesak Day            – Fri 1 May 2026 (merged; additional lieu day given)
  7. Hari Raya Puasa      – Wed 13 May 2026
  8. National Day         – Mon 10 Aug 2026
  9. Hari Raya Haji       – Sun 13 Sep 2026 (mon 14 Sep in lieu)
 10. Deepavali            – Sat 17 Oct 2026 (mon 19 Oct in lieu)
 11. Christmas Day        – Fri 25 Dec 2026

Where a public holiday falls on a Saturday, the following Monday is
designated as a day off in lieu. Where it falls on a Sunday, the following
Monday is designated as a public holiday (per gazette).

Employees required to work on a public holiday (must be approved in advance
by the department head) are entitled to either:
  (a) One additional day of paid leave per public holiday worked, OR
  (b) 2.0x basic hourly rate of pay for hours worked (EA-covered employees)

Employees in regional offices observe local public holidays in their country
of employment; Singapore public holidays do not apply to overseas offices.

3.7 Business Travel
Employees travelling for business purposes must submit a Travel Request
Form (TR-01) a minimum of 7 days in advance (21 days for international
travel) for approval by the line manager and Finance. The Travel Policy
(Finance Handbook Section 3) governs booking classes, per-diem rates, and
allowances. The key principles are:

Flights:
  - Economy for flights < 6 hours duration
  - Premium Economy or Business for flights >= 6 hours (manager discretion)
  - Business Class for Grade D and above on all flights >= 4 hours

Accommodation:
  - Standard single room at approved corporate hotel partners
  - Maximum nightly rate: S$250 Singapore / APAC; S$300 EMEA / Americas

Per diems (incidentals, meals, local transport):
  - S$80 per day Singapore
  - S$120 per day APAC (major cities)
  - S$150 per day EMEA / Americas

All travel must be booked through the corporate travel agent (Travelstop).
Personal extensions to business travel are permitted at the employee's own
expense, subject to not impacting business commitments and line manager
approval. Travel insurance is provided automatically for all business trips
via the company's group travel policy with AIG Asia.

3.8 Leave-of-Absence (Unpaid)
Employees may apply for unpaid leave of absence for up to 3 months for
reasons including (but not limited to) personal development, study, caregiving,
volunteering, or extended travel. Applications must be made on Form LUA-01
at least 30 days in advance and require:
  - Line manager endorsement
  - Department head (Grade D+) approval
  - Final sign-off by People Operations

During unpaid leave:
  - Salary is suspended; no AWS or bonus pro-rating accrues
  - CPF contributions are not made by either party
  - Leave entitlements (annual, sick) do not accrue
  - ESOP vesting continues (per ESOP agreement)
  - Group insurance cover continues for up to 3 months

Employment seniority is preserved during periods of approved unpaid leave
less than 6 months per rolling 24-month period.

SECTION 4: LEAVE ENTITLEMENTS
================================================================================

4.1 Leave Framework Overview
DBizLabs provides a generous and comprehensive leave programme designed to
support work-life balance, rest, recovery, and major life events. All leave
entitlements below are minimums; individual managers may, at their sole
discretion, approve additional unpaid leave beyond these statutory and
company minima.

Leave is administered via the Workday HCM platform. Employees are responsible
for submitting leave requests in advance where possible and for ensuring
appropriate handover and coverage before going on leave.

For every leave type, the following ground rules apply:
  (a) Leave requests must be submitted via Workday by the employee.
  (b) Leave of 2 days or less requires line manager approval.
  (c) Leave of 3–9 days requires line manager + department head approval.
  (d) Leave of 10+ days requires line manager + department head + HRBP.
  (e) Any leave concurrent with critical business periods (e.g. financial
      year-end, product launches, major customer go-live) additionally
      requires CFO or COO sign-off.
  (f) Retroactive leave requests (submitted after the absence has begun)
      require written justification and HRBP approval.
  (g) All leave balances are available on the Workday Employee Self-Service
      portal and are updated daily.

4.2 Annual Leave Entitlement
Annual leave entitlement varies by employee grade as at the beginning of the
leave year (1 January) and is as follows:

Grade A  : 18 working days per calendar year
Grade B  : 22 working days per calendar year
Grade C  : 25 working days per calendar year
Grade C+ : 25 working days per calendar year
Grade D  : 28 working days per calendar year
Grade E  : 30 working days per calendar year
Grade E+ : 30 working days per calendar year (plus additional 5 days at
           the CEO's discretion, managed outside Workday)

Note that the calendar year is used for annual leave (not the fiscal year).
Part-Time employees receive a pro-rata portion of the above based on
contracted weekly hours / 44 hours. Contract employees receive 7% of gross
annual salary as leave encashment (per the Employment Act) unless their
contract specifies otherwise.

4.3 Long-Service Annual Leave Enhancement
In addition to the base annual leave entitlement above, employees who have
completed 5 years of continuous service with DBizLabs (or with a predecessor
company acquired by DBizLabs, at the company's discretion) receive:
  - +5 days of additional annual leave, effective 1 January following the
    5-year anniversary.

Thereafter, the additional leave balance increases by +1 day per completed
year of service (so 6 years = +6 days, 7 years = +7 days, etc.) up to a
maximum additional cap of 30 additional days.

In practical terms this means:
  - A Grade B employee at 5 years: 22 + 5 = 27 days
  - A Grade B employee at 10 years: 22 + 10 = 32 days
  - A Grade B employee at 25+ years: 22 + 30 = 52 days (maximum cap)

Long-service leave additions are automatic and applied by People Operations
in January of each year; no application is required.

4.4 Annual Leave Carry-Forward and Encashment Policy
Leave year: 1 January to 31 December.

Up to 50% of annual leave entitlement may be carried forward to the next
year, subject to a maximum carry-forward cap of:
  - Grade A:  9 days carry-forward cap
  - Grade B:  11 days carry-forward cap
  - Grade C:  13 days carry-forward cap
  - Grade C+: 13 days carry-forward cap
  - Grade D:  14 days carry-forward cap
  - Grade E:  15 days carry-forward cap
  - Grade E+: 15 days carry-forward cap

Any annual leave above this cap that has not been taken by 31 December will
be automatically encashed in the first payroll of January at the employee's
current basic daily rate of pay. Encashment is subject to CPF deductions
(where applicable) and normal income tax.

Managers are responsible for ensuring that their team members take adequate
leave throughout the year. The HR team publishes leave utilisation reports
quarterly; any employee who has taken fewer than 5 days of annual leave by
30 June will be contacted by their HRBP to discuss and encourage leave
planning. "Use-it-or-lose-it" does not apply within the 50% cap, but
encouraging rest is a cultural priority.

4.5 Sick Leave Entitlement
All Full-Time and Part-Time permanent employees are entitled to:

  (a) Outpatient Sick Leave: 14 days per calendar year, with no waiting
      period from the date of joining.
  (b) Hospitalisation Sick Leave: Up to 60 working days per calendar year,
      of which the first 30 days are paid at 100% of basic salary, and the
      remaining 30 days at 66.67% of basic salary. Hospitalisation leave
      includes quarantine orders and day surgeries.

Sick leave may be used for:
  - Personal illness, injury, or medical appointments that cannot be
    scheduled outside working hours
  - Quarantine orders (SHN) issued by MOH or equivalent overseas authority
  - Accompanying a child or spouse for a hospital admission or surgery
    (maximum 5 days per year; considered "caregiver sick leave" and drawn
    from the 14-day outpatient pool)

Medical certificates are required:
  - For any sick leave of 2 days or more in a row
  - For any sick leave taken on a Monday or Friday (to avoid long-weekend
    abuse — although the policy is applied with common sense; HR may waive
    this requirement at its discretion)
  - For any hospitalisation leave regardless of duration

MCs must be submitted via Workday within 3 working days of the absence.
Uncertified sick leave (no MC) of 1 day per month is permitted on a
self-declaration basis via Form SL-01. Employees exceeding 6 self-declared
sick days per year will be required to attend a meeting with the HRBP and
company-appointed panel doctor.

4.6 Maternity Leave
All female employees (regardless of nationality, employment pass status, or
length of service) are entitled to 16 weeks of paid Maternity Leave, which
is a benefit above and beyond the statutory 16 weeks mandated under the
Singapore Employment Act for eligible citizens. Government-Paid Maternity
Benefit (GPMB) rules apply where the employee is eligible (Singapore citizen
mothers, 3rd+ children etc.).

Distribution of the 16 weeks:
  - Minimum 4 weeks must be taken immediately after confinement
  - Remaining 12 weeks may be taken flexibly within 12 months of the birth
    date, subject to manager approval and child-care arrangements
  - Flexi-taking must be pre-approved via Form ML-02 and clearly documented

Maternity Leave is paid at the employee's current gross monthly salary
(DBizLabs tops up the GPMB amount to ensure full salary continuity).

Pre-natal leave: 2 days of paid pre-natal examination leave per pregnancy,
available from the 2nd trimester onwards, in addition to the 16-week
entitlement. These 2 days are not drawn from annual leave or sick leave.

Pregnancy loss / stillbirth: Up to 8 weeks of paid leave for miscarriage
occurring after week 12 of pregnancy, or 2 weeks of paid leave for early
pregnancy loss before week 12. Supported by a doctor's letter.

4.7 Paternity Leave
All male employees (regardless of nationality) are entitled to 2 weeks of
paid Paternity Leave following the birth of their child. This must be taken
within 16 weeks of the birth date and may be:
  - Taken as a single 2-week block, OR
  - Split into 10 individual days taken flexibly within 12 months of birth,
    subject to manager approval via Form PL-01

Paternity Leave is paid at 100% of gross monthly salary. Government-Paid
Paternity Benefit (GPPB) applies for Singaporean fathers where eligible;
DBizLabs tops up the balance to ensure no loss of salary.

4.8 Adoption Leave and Surrogacy
Adoptive parents are entitled to:
  - Primary adopter: 12 weeks of paid leave (matching maternity framework
    where applicable)
  - Secondary adopter: 2 weeks of paid leave (matching paternity framework)

Leave is available following official court placement order and may be used
for bonding and settling-in period. Supported by adoption court documents.
Surrogacy arrangements are treated on a case-by-case basis by People
Operations with reference to the Adoption of Children Act and any applicable
Ministry of Social and Family Development guidelines.

4.9 Compassionate Leave (Bereavement Leave)
In the unfortunate event of the death of an immediate family member,
employees are entitled to 5 working days of paid Compassionate Leave per
occurrence. Immediate family is defined as:
  - Spouse / civil partner / de-facto partner
  - Children (biological, adopted, step, foster)
  - Parents and parents-in-law
  - Siblings (biological, adopted, step, half, in-law)
  - Grandparents and grandparents-in-law

In the event of the death of a grandchild, aunt, uncle, niece, nephew, or
cousin, employees are entitled to 3 working days of paid compassionate leave.
Additional unpaid leave may be granted upon request at the manager's
discretion.

Compassionate leave does not require a death certificate as a condition of
approval; however, a copy may be requested after the fact for audit purposes.
Managers are instructed to be generous and flexible in granting additional
time where the employee needs it; the company's values always take precedence
over strict policy interpretation.

4.10 Marriage Leave
Employees getting married (to any gender, any jurisdiction) are entitled to

marriage certificate). Marriage leave may be combined with annual leave for

is no lifetime cap. However, marriage leave cannot be taken retroactively
for marriages that occurred before the employee joined DBizLabs.

4.14 Voting, Jury Service, and Public Duties
Employees who are eligible to vote in Singapore General Elections or

5.3 13th Month Bonus (Annual Wage Supplement — AWS)
DBizLabs provides a 13th-month bonus, more commonly known as the Annual
Wage Supplement (AWS). The AWS is pro-rated for employees who joined or
left during the calendar year, based on the number of complete calendar
months in employment. The formula is:

  AWS = (Monthly Base Salary as at December) × (Completed Months / 12)

Where the company's full-year financial performance is above internal plan,
the AWS may be increased from 1.0 month to a maximum of 2.0 months at the
discretion of the Board of Directors. AWS is paid together with the
December payroll on or about 20 December each year. Employees who resign
or are terminated before 1 December of the relevant year are NOT eligible
for a pro-rated AWS.

5.4 Performance Bonus Scheme (Annual Variable Component)
In addition to the AWS, DBizLabs operates a performance-based Annual
Variable Bonus plan (commonly referred to as the "Performance Bonus")
targeting between 0 and 3 additional months of base salary. Actual payout
is determined by a combination of:

  (a) COMPANY PERFORMANCE (40% of the weighting): Based on achievement of
      full-year revenue, EBITDA, and strategic milestones as approved by
      the Board at the beginning of the year.
      - Exceeds plan (110%+): Company factor = 1.5x
      - Meets plan (95–109%): Company factor = 1.0x
      - Below plan (80–94%): Company factor = 0.5x
      - Significantly below (<80%): Company factor = 0x

  (b) INDIVIDUAL PERFORMANCE (60% of the weighting): Based on the
      individual's annual performance rating (see Section 6).
      - Rating 5 (Exceptional): Individual factor = 2.0x
      - Rating 4 (Exceeds Expectations): Individual factor = 1.3x
      - Rating 3 (Meets Expectations): Individual factor = 1.0x
      - Rating 2 (Needs Improvement): Individual factor = 0.25x
      - Rating 1 (Unsatisfactory / PIP): Individual factor = 0x

Final bonus (in months of base salary):

  Target Bonus  ×  Company Factor ×  Individual Factor

Target bonus by grade (months of base):
  Grade A  :  1.5 months target
  Grade B  :  2.0 months target
  Grade C  :  2.5 months target
  Grade C+ :  3.0 months target
  Grade D  :  3.0 months target
  Grade E  :  3.0 months target (higher via Long-Term Incentive scheme)
  Grade E+ :  Discretionary scheme approved separately by RemCo

Example: Grade C employee with target bonus of 2.5 months, company meets plan
(1.0x), individual Rating 4 (1.3x) → 2.5 × 1.0 × 1.3 = 3.25 months bonus.

The performance bonus is paid together with the March payroll of the
following year, subject to the employee being active on the payroll on the
date of payment. Employees who resign or are terminated before the payment
date are not eligible for the performance bonus (except in cases of
redundancy, where the Board may approve a pro-rated amount).

5.5 Sales Commission Plan (Go-To-Market Roles Only)

5.15 Employee Stock Option Plan (ESOP)

Eligibility:

SECTION 6: PERFORMANCE MANAGEMENT, PROMOTION & PIP
================================================================================

6.1 Performance Management Philosophy
The performance management system at DBizLabs is designed to:
  - Align individual goals with team, department, and company OKRs
  - Provide continuous, timely, and actionable feedback — not a once-a-year
    compliance exercise
  - Support employee growth, career development, and promotion
  - Differentiate compensation fairly based on impact delivered
  - Identify underperformance early and provide structured support

6.2 Quarterly Check-Ins
Formal performance conversations occur every quarter, on the following
schedule:
  - Q1 Check-in: Mid-April (covers Jan–Mar)
  - Q2 Check-in: Mid-July (covers Apr–Jun)
  - Q3 Check-in: Mid-October (covers Jul–Sep)
  - Q4 Annual Review: Mid-January (covers full calendar year; weighting
    applies — see below)

In addition to these formal sessions, managers are expected to hold weekly
1:1 meetings with each direct report (minimum 30 minutes per week, with
agenda set collaboratively between manager and employee). Skipped 1:1s are
tracked by the People Analytics team and flagged to HR if a manager has
fewer than 40% of scheduled 1:1s in any given month.

6.3 Annual Review Weighting
The Q4 Annual Review aggregates performance across the full year. The
weighting applied to each quarter's performance in determining the final
annual rating is:

  Q1 (January–March):   30% weighting
  Q2 (April–June):      20% weighting
  Q3 (July–September):  20% weighting
  Q4 (October–December): 30% weighting

This structure intentionally gives heavier weight to the first and last
quarters (typically when goals are set and when annual outcomes are
realised) while acknowledging the mid-year contribution of Q2 and Q3.

6.4 Performance Rating Scale (1–5)
The annual performance is rated on a 5-point scale:

Rating 5: EXCEPTIONAL
  - Consistently and significantly exceeds expectations across all
    dimensions of the role. Delivers impact that is 2–3x the level
    expected for the grade. Often transforms the team or product area.
  - Typically achieved by less than 8% of employees in any given cycle.
  - Automatic short-listing for promotion (if eligible) and maximum bonus.

Rating 4: EXCEEDS EXPECTATIONS
  - Consistently delivers above the expectations of the role across most
    or all dimensions. Quality of work is high, and there is evidence of
    proactivity, ownership, and stretch.
  - Typically achieved by 22–28% of employees.
  - Eligible for strong bonus, accelerated L&D budget, and promotion
    consideration if maintained for 2+ cycles.

Rating 3: MEETS EXPECTATIONS
  - Fully meets the requirements of the role. Work is delivered on time,
    to the required standard, without the need for excessive supervision.
    "Meets Expectations" is a solid, good rating reflecting a reliable
    performer who is doing their job well.
  - Typically the largest cohort: 50–60% of employees.
  - Eligible for full target bonus, standard annual salary adjustment,
    and normal L&D budget.

Rating 2: NEEDS IMPROVEMENT
  - Performance in some areas falls below the expectations of the role.
    While there are positive contributions, there are gaps in quality,
    delivery, impact, or behaviour that require focused attention.
  - Typically 8–12% of employees.
  - Triggers the creation of a 90-day Performance Improvement Plan (PIP)
    if not already in place; bonus may be significantly reduced or zero.

Rating 1: UNSATISFACTORY (on PIP or equivalent)
  - Performance is materially and consistently below the requirements of
    the role, with little or no improvement despite feedback and support.
    Alternatively, there is a serious single incident of misconduct or
    negligence that warrants immediate disciplinary action.
  - Typically 2–4% of employees.
  - Active PIP in place with documented performance goals. If the PIP is
    not passed, the employment relationship will be terminated.

6.5 Calibration and Forced Distribution
Managers meet at the end of each cycle in a calibration session facilitated
by the HRBP to ensure consistency of ratings across teams and departments.
A loose forced-distribution guideline is applied at the department level:

  Rating 5:  5–10%
  Rating 4:  20–30%
  Rating 3:  50–65%
  Rating 2:  5–12%
  Rating 1:  1–5%

These bands are guidance, not rigid rules. The Remuneration Committee may
approve exceptions for high-performing departments or specific mission-
critical teams.

6.6 Promotion Criteria Matrix
Promotions follow a structured assessment framework. The minimum time in
current grade before being eligible for promotion is:

  Grade A  → B  : 18 months at A (or 12 months with Rating 5 x2)
  Grade B  → C  : 24 months at B (or 18 months with Rating 5 x2)
  Grade C  → C+ : 24 months at C (with at least 1 Rating 5 and 1 Rating 4)
  Grade C+ → D  : 30 months at C+ (with at least 2 Rating 4 and 1 Rating 5)
  Grade D  → E  : 36 months at D (with RemCo approval; headcount-limited)
  Grade E  → E+ : Discretion of CEO and Chairman (no fixed schedule)

The Promotion Assessment Criteria matrix (Form PRO-09) covers 5 dimensions:

Dimension 1: TECHNICAL / FUNCTIONAL EXPERTISE
  - Evidence of expertise in the core skills of the role
  - Contribution to knowledge sharing, documentation, and up-skilling others
  - Reputation as a go-to person within the function or domain

Dimension 2: IMPACT AND BUSINESS RESULTS
  - Quantitative metrics demonstrating impact (OKR achievement, revenue
    contributed, products shipped, incidents resolved, etc.)
  - Scope of impact: team-level → department-level → company-level

Dimension 3: LEADERSHIP AND COLLABORATION
  - Ability to lead projects and initiatives (even without formal reports)
  - Influence across teams and functions
  - Mentorship and development of more junior team members
  - Exemplification of DBizLabs cultural values

Dimension 4: STRATEGIC THINKING AND JUDGEMENT
  - Ability to see the big picture and make decisions aligned with long-term
    company interests
  - Quality of judgement under uncertainty and with incomplete information
  - Understanding of trade-offs between speed, cost, quality, and risk

Dimension 5: COMMUNICATION AND STAKEHOLDER MANAGEMENT
  - Clarity of written and verbal communication
  - Ability to communicate with executives, customers, and cross-functional
    stakeholders
  - Ability to persuade and influence without authority

Each dimension is scored 1–5 with a required minimum of "4" on the primary
dimensions for promotion to the next grade. Full scoring rubrics with
behavioural indicators are available on the People Portal in Workday.

6.7 Performance Improvement Plan (PIP)
Where an employee's performance is rated at 1 or has been at 2 for 2
consecutive cycles with insufficient progress, the manager, in consultation
with the HRBP and Legal, will initiate a Performance Improvement Plan (PIP).

The standard PIP duration is 90 calendar days from the date of the PIP
kick-off meeting. A PIP document will set out in writing:
  (a) The specific areas of underperformance (with examples and evidence)
  (b) Clearly defined, measurable, and time-bound performance objectives
      (typically 3–5 SMART goals)
  (c) The support, resources, and training that will be provided by the
      manager and the company (e.g. a dedicated mentor, targeted training,
      reduced scope of work to focus on development, weekly check-ins)
  (d) The frequency and format of formal check-ins (minimum weekly 1:1
      with manager, bi-weekly with HRBP)
  (e) The consequences of not meeting the PIP goals, which will typically
      be termination of employment with appropriate notice or pay in lieu.

At the end of the 90-day PIP period, one of three outcomes occurs:
  (a) PASS: Performance has improved to "Meets Expectations" (Rating 3) or
      above. The PIP is closed and the employee returns to the standard
      performance management cycle. A follow-up review at 6 months is
      recommended to ensure sustained improvement.
  (b) EXTEND: Significant and genuine progress has been made, but more time
      is needed to fully meet all objectives. Extension is permitted ONCE
      for up to 30 additional days, with RemCo sign-off.
  (c) FAIL: Performance has not improved to the required standard. The
      employment relationship is terminated in accordance with the notice
      period set out in Appendix B. Termination payments and any severance
      will be handled in strict compliance with the Employment Act.

6.8 Salary Adjustments
Base salary adjustments are considered annually as part of the Q1
compensation review cycle (effective 1 April each year). Adjustments are
driven by:
  (a) Performance rating in the preceding calendar year
  (b) Position of current salary within the grade band (Appendix A)
  (c) External market movement based on Radford/Aon surveys
  (d) Promotion (effective from first day of the month following approval)

Mid-cycle salary adjustments (outside the April cycle) are permitted only
in the following exceptional circumstances:
  - Internal promotion (see above)
  - Counter-offer retention in the face of a documented external offer
    (CFO approval required)
  - Major change in role or scope of responsibilities
  - Correction of a documented pay inequity (HRBP + Legal sign-off)

6.9 Career Pathing and Dual Ladders
DBizLabs maintains dual career ladders: the Individual Contributor (IC)
track and the People Manager (leadership) track. Employees may progress
equivalently through either track without being required to manage people
if that is not their strength or preference.

IC track levels map to grades:
  Junior IC → Grade A
  Senior IC → Grade B
  Staff IC → Grade C
  Senior Staff IC → Grade C+
  Principal IC → Grade D
  Distinguished IC → Grade E
  Fellow → Grade E+

People Manager track maps to grades:
  Team Lead (4–8 reports) → Grade C / C+
  Department Head (15–40 reports) → Grade D
  VP / CXO (50+ reports, P&L owner) → Grade E / E+

Internal mobility across tracks and teams is actively encouraged; 21% of
employees changed teams internally in FY2025, and the company targets 25%
internal mobility in FY2026.

SECTION 7: ETHICS, INTEGRITY & CODE OF CONDUCT
================================================================================

7.1 Our Commitment to Doing Business the Right Way
Integrity is the first of DBizLabs's five core values. We are committed to
conducting business ethically, transparently, and in full compliance with
all applicable laws, regulations, and professional standards in every
jurisdiction where we operate — including, without limitation, the Singapore
Prevention of Corruption Act (POCA), the US Foreign Corrupt Practices Act
(FCPA), the UK Bribery Act 2010, and the Singapore AI Governance Act 2026.

This Section 7 sets out standards of ethical behaviour for every employee,
contractor, director, and agent of DBizLabs. Breaches of this Code of
Conduct may result in disciplinary action up to and including termination
of employment, reporting to the relevant authorities, and civil or criminal
proceedings as appropriate. There is zero tolerance for retaliation against
any employee who raises a genuine concern in good faith.

7.2 Anti-Bribery and Anti-Corruption Policy
DBizLabs strictly prohibits the offering, promising, giving, requesting,
or accepting of any bribe, kickback, or improper payment in any form,
whether in dealings with government officials, private sector counterparts,
customers, suppliers, or any third party.

7.2.1 Gifts, Hospitality, and Entertainment
The following framework applies to gifts, meals, hospitality, and
entertainment given or received by any employee in the course of business.
The key principle is "no strings attached" — any benefit given or received
must be a normal business courtesy, proportionate, and clearly not intended
to influence any business decision.

THRESHOLD 1: Below S$150 per occurrence (per individual, per event)
  - Automatically acceptable. No pre-approval required.
  - Must still be recorded in the Gifts & Hospitality Register
    (Form GHR-01) within 5 business days if value > S$100, or if given/
    received from a government official at any value.
  - Examples: Coffee with a vendor (S$8), box of cookies from a customer
    during Deepavali (S$65), client lunch at a hawker centre (S$35/pax).

THRESHOLD 2: S$150.01 to S$1,000 per occurrence
  - Permitted ONLY with the PRIOR WRITTEN APPROVAL of the employee's line
    manager.
  - Must be recorded in the Gifts & Hospitality Register within 5 business
    days of the event.
  - Typical examples: Client dinner at a restaurant (S$280/person), tickets
    to a local football match (S$420/ticket), conference registration
    sponsored by a vendor (S$890).

THRESHOLD 3: Above S$1,000 per occurrence
  - Permitted ONLY with the PRIOR WRITTEN APPROVAL of the CHIEF FINANCIAL
    OFFICER (Karthiga Rajoo) or, in their absence, the Chief Operating
    Officer (Divya Muthu).
  - Must be recorded in the Gifts & Hospitality Register and additionally
    reported to the Audit & Risk Committee at its next meeting.
  - Examples: Overseas conference sponsorship including flights (S$2,200),
    premium Singapore Grand Prix hospitality package (S$5,800/pax),
    speaking engagement fee (S$3,000 honorarium).

7.2.2 Facilitation Payments and "Grease" Payments
DBizLabs prohibits facilitation payments (also called "grease payments"),
i.e. small payments to low-level government officials to speed up routine,
non-discretionary actions (such as customs clearance, visa processing, or
utility connections). If an employee is placed in a situation where a
facilitation payment appears to be demanded, the employee must:
  (a) Decline the demand firmly but politely;
  (b) Immediately escalate to Legal (Santhana Krishnan, Head of Legal) and
      the CFO;
  (c) Document the interaction in full;
  (d) Under no circumstances make the payment personally and seek
      reimbursement, as doing so constitutes a policy breach.

If personal safety is threatened, the employee may make the payment under
duress but must report the incident to Legal within 24 hours for proper
reporting and remediation.

7.2.3 Gifts and Hospitality to Government Officials
Any gift given to or received from a government official (including
employees of statutory boards, government-linked companies with >=20%
government shareholding, public universities, and political office-holders
or political parties) must be escalated to Legal for sign-off REGARDLESS
OF VALUE. The legal team will assess whether the transaction is compliant
with POCA, the FCPA, and the UK Bribery Act before the gift may be given
or accepted.

7.3 Gifts Register (Declaration Requirement)
All gifts, hospitality, and entertainment (whether given or received) that
meet any of the following criteria must be declared in the central Gifts &
Hospitality Register maintained by the Finance Operations team via Form
GHR-01 within 5 business days of the event:
  (a) Value exceeds S$100 per person per occurrence
  (b) Involves a government official, political party, or candidate for
      political office (at any value, even a S$5 coffee)
  (c) Is part of a pattern of multiple small gifts to the same person or
      organisation aggregating to more than S$300 in any calendar quarter
  (d) Takes place outside the employee's home country (any value)

The register is reviewed quarterly by Internal Audit and semi-annually by
the Audit & Risk Committee. Failure to declare a reportable gift or
hospitality event is a disciplinary offence, regardless of the value or
intent of the gift.

7.4 Conflicts of Interest
Every employee, contractor, and director of DBizLabs has a duty to act in
the best interests of the company and to avoid situations where a personal
interest, relationship, or outside activity conflicts — or could reasonably
appear to conflict — with the interests of DBizLabs.

7.4.1 Annual Disclosure Requirement
All employees (Grade A through E+) must complete and submit the Conflict
of Interest Annual Declaration (Form COI-01) via Workday by 31 January of
each year, covering the preceding calendar year. The form requires
disclosure of:
  (a) Any directorship, partnership, or shareholding (>=5%) held in any
      other company, whether public or private
  (b) Any paid or unpaid outside employment, consultancy, advisory role,
      or board membership
  (c) Any close personal relationship (family member, domestic partner,
      close friend) with any supplier, customer, competitor, or job
      candidate involved in a decision the employee may influence
  (d) Any personal transaction or financial relationship with DBizLabs
      (including the Housing Loan benefit)
  (e) Any ownership of real property or other assets that could create a
      conflict with DBizLabs activities

New joiners must complete an initial COI declaration as part of Day 1
onboarding. Any material change to circumstances during the year must be
reported via an amended Form COI-01 within 10 business days of the change.

7.4.2 Examples of Conflicts Requiring Disclosure and Management
  - An engineer contributing open-source code to a project owned by a
    direct competitor of DBizLabs (e.g. another MES vendor, another HR
    SaaS platform)
  - A procurement specialist whose sibling is the Sales Director of a
    software vendor bidding on a DBizLabs RFP
  - A Sales Executive who moonlights on weekends selling a CRM product
    that competes indirectly with Hirethm's capabilities
  - A Finance manager with a S$80,000 investment in a cryptocurrency fund
    that is also a potential Bond Copilot customer
  - A Recruiter whose spouse is a candidate for a Senior Data Scientist
    position on a team where the Recruiter is the recruiter of record

In all such cases, the conflict does not necessarily prevent the employee
from continuing in their role; appropriate management strategies (such as
recusal from relevant decisions, information barriers, or reassignment of
responsibilities) will be put in place by the HRBP and Legal. Deliberate
non-disclosure of a material conflict, however, is a serious disciplinary
offence.

7.5 Anti-Harassment, Anti-Discrimination, and Dignity at Work
DBizLabs is committed to maintaining a workplace that is free from
harassment, discrimination, bullying, and victimisation. We treat this as
both a legal obligation and a cultural imperative. This policy applies
equally to conduct between employees, and to conduct by or directed at
customers, contractors, visitors, and members of the public in connection
with work.

Prohibited conduct includes (but is not limited to):
  (a) SEXUAL HARASSMENT: Unwelcome sexual advances, requests for sexual
      favours, and other verbal or physical conduct of a sexual nature,
      whether explicit or implicit, including the creation of a hostile
      work environment.
  (b) VERBAL AND PHYSICAL HARASSMENT: Insults, slurs, epithets, name-
      calling, threatening or intimidating behaviour, physical touching,
      blocking movement, or abusive language.
  (c) BULLYING: Persistent, unreasonable, and aggressive behaviour
      directed at an individual or group, including excessive micromanage-
      ment, setting up for failure, spreading rumours, or social exclusion.
  (d) DISCRIMINATION: Less favourable treatment on the basis of any of the
      protected characteristics listed in Section 7.9 below.

7.5.1 Reporting and Escalation Paths for Harassment
An employee who experiences, witnesses, or becomes aware of conduct that
they believe may constitute harassment or bullying should take action as
soon as possible. Any of the following channels may be used, based on the
employee's comfort:

  (a) Informal resolution with the perpetrator (only if the employee feels
      safe doing so): A direct, respectful conversation stating the impact
      of the behaviour and requesting it stop.
  (b) Line manager: If trust exists. Managers are required to escalate all
      reports to HR within 24 hours regardless of the manager's personal
      view of the situation.
  (c) HR Business Partner (HRBP): The primary confidential channel. All
      reports made to HR are treated confidentially to the extent possible
      while ensuring a fair and thorough investigation.
  (d) Whistleblower Channel (Section 7.7): For cases involving senior
      management, directors, or where the employee fears retaliation for
      reporting via other channels.
  (e) External agencies (Tafep, MOM, police) where the conduct constitutes
      a criminal offence.

All reports are investigated promptly, thoroughly, and impartially by a
trained investigator (typically an external third-party for reports
involving Grade D+ or where there are perceived conflicts of interest).
Retaliation against any person who makes a good-faith report is strictly
prohibited and will itself be subject to disciplinary action up to and
including termination.

7.6 Confidentiality and Non-Disclosure Obligations
Every employee has an obligation to protect the confidential information
of DBizLabs, its customers, its suppliers, and its partners. This

Confidential information includes, without limitation:

The confidentiality obligation survives termination of employment for ANY

  - TIER 2 (CONFIDENTIAL): Customer data, financial data below Board

  - TIER 3 (RESTRICTED): Board papers, individual salary data, source

      all access; external sharing only with CEO / CFO sign-off.

7.12 Disciplinary Procedure
Alleged breaches of this Code of Conduct, company policy, or the terms of
employment will be dealt with using the following graduated disciplinary
procedure (except in cases of serious misconduct where summary dismissal
is warranted):

Step 1: Verbal Counselling. Informal feedback from the line manager or HRBP.
  Documented only in the manager's notes, not the permanent personnel file.

Step 2: Written Warning. A formal letter setting out the breach, required
  improvements, and a timeline (typically 30–60 days) for improvement.
  Countersigned by the employee and HRBP. Placed on the personnel file and
  active for 12 months.

Step 3: Final Written Warning. Issued if a breach is repeated within the
  active period of a written warning, or for a more serious first offence.
  States that any further breach may result in termination. Active for
  24 months.

Step 4: Termination of Employment. With appropriate notice or pay in lieu
  (Appendix B) for breaches that are not sufficiently serious for summary
  dismissal, or for failure to improve following a Final Written Warning.

Serious misconduct (including but not limited to: fraud, theft, physical
assault, deliberate breach of the anti-bribery policy, intentional misuse
of customer data, material breach of the NDA, or unlawful discrimination)
warrants immediate summary dismissal without notice or severance, and
where applicable reporting to the relevant law enforcement authorities.

Employees have the right to be accompanied at formal disciplinary hearings
by a fellow employee or trade union representative of their choice.

8.1 Our Commitment to Workplace Safety and Health
The health, safety, and wellbeing of every DBizLabs team member — whether
working in the office, from home, at customer sites, or on business travel
— is a fundamental priority for the company. DBizLabs fully complies with
the Singapore Workplace Safety and Health Act (WSHA), and we maintain a
workplace safety and health management system (WSHMS) aligned to ISO 45001.

The overall responsibility for WSH policy rests with the COO, Divya Muthu,
supported by the WSH Committee which meets monthly (see Section 8.4). The
target is zero workplace fatalities, zero cases of occupational disease,
and an LTIR (Lost Time Injury Rate) of < 0.5 per 100 full-time employees
per year. As of Q3 FY2026, the 3-year rolling LTIR is 0.18, which is well
below the industry benchmark for software companies.

8.3 Office Ergonomics and DSE (Display Screen Equipment) Assessments

8.5 Remote Work Safety Checklist (Home Office Safety)
Employees working remotely (up to 2 days per week per Section 3.3) are
responsible for ensuring their home workspace is safe and ergonomic. The
following checklist must be self-completed by each remote employee at the
start of employment and reconfirmed annually:

8.5.1 Electrical Safety
  - Desk and equipment are positioned away from water sources (kitchen
    sinks, water dispensers, bathrooms)
  - No loose or damaged electrical cords, overloaded power sockets, or
    daisy-chained multiplugs (use surge-protected power strips only)
  - Laptop and charger are placed on a hard, flat surface — never on a
    bed, sofa, or pile of clothing (which can block vents and cause
    overheating or fire risk)
  - Smoke detector and, where applicable, circuit breaker for the home
    office area are in working order

8.5.2 Ergonomics (Home Office)
  - Chair provides adequate back support (lumbar) and is set at a height
    such that the employee's elbows are at 90 degrees when typing
  - Feet are flat on the floor or supported by a footrest
  - Top of the monitor is at or just below eye level, approximately 50cm
    from the face.
  - Keyboard and mouse are positioned close to the employee to avoid
    over-reaching
  - Adequate lighting to avoid glare and reflections on the screen

8.5.3 Slips, Trips, and Falls
  - Cables and wires are routed along the wall or secured with cable
    ties; no cables crossing walkways where someone might trip
  - Floor is clear of clutter, boxes, and loose rugs (particularly near
    the work area and any escape route)
  - Stairs (if any) are free from objects

8.5.4 Fire and Escape
  - Workspace has a clear, unobstructed escape route to the main exit of
    the residence
  - Smoke alarm present within 3 metres of the home office area, with
    working batteries
  - The employee knows the emergency evacuation procedure of the
    residential building

If an employee identifies an unsafe condition in their home workspace that
cannot be resolved with a minor adjustment, they should:
  1. Stop working from home immediately
  2. Book a hot-desk in the Marina One office for the affected days
  3. Notify People Operations for a remote safety assessment visit
     (covered by the company)

8.6 Information Security and Data Privacy (Remote Work)
In addition to physical safety, employees working remotely must:
  (a) Use ONLY the company-provided laptop or approved BYOD device for
      work purposes. Personal laptops, tablets, and home PCs are strictly
      prohibited from accessing DBizLabs Tier 2 and Tier 3 data.
  (b) Connect to the company network via the corporate VPN (Palo Alto
      GlobalProtect with multi-factor authentication) for any access to
      internal systems, customer data, or production environments. Split
      tunnelling is disabled by default.
  (c) Never work on confidential materials in public spaces (cafes,
      airports, co-working hot-desk floors) where a privacy screen is not
      used and shoulder-surfing could occur.
  (d) Never leave a laptop unlocked and unattended even for 30 seconds;
      the laptop auto-lock policy is 2 minutes of inactivity, enforced
      via Intune / JAMF.
  (e) Hold work-related video calls only from a private area where
      conversations cannot be overheard by household members or visitors.
      Headsets with noise cancelling microphones must be used.

8.9 Data Privacy (Personal Data Protection Act — PDPA)
DBizLabs is a Data Controller subject to the Singapore Personal Data
Protection Act 2012 (PDPA, as amended 2020 and 2024), and additionally the
EU GDPR for data of EEA-based employees and customers, and the California
CPRA for California-based individuals. The Chief Data Protection Officer
(CDPO) is Santhana Krishnan, Head of Legal, reachable at dpo@dbizlabs.ai.

Key obligations of employees under PDPA:
  (a) COLLECT ONLY WHAT YOU NEED: Collect only personal data that is
      adequate, relevant, and necessary for the stated purpose. For
      example, Hirethm recruiters do not need to collect information
      about a candidate's religious beliefs or medical history for most
      roles.
  (b) NOTICE AND CONSENT: Individuals must be informed of the purpose(s)
      for which their personal data is being collected, used, or disclosed
      — either via a privacy policy, a recruitment privacy notice, or a
      customer DPA. Explicit consent must be obtained for sensitive
      personal data (race, religion, health, biometrics, sexual
      orientation, etc.) unless an exception applies.
  (c) PURPOSE LIMITATION: Do not use personal data for a purpose other
      than the one for which it was originally collected, unless a new
      consent is obtained or a PDPA exception applies. For example, a
      customer's mobile phone number collected for billing purposes should
      not be added to a marketing WhatsApp list.
  (d) ACCESS AND CORRECTION: Individuals have the right to request access
      to and correction of their personal data held by DBizLabs. All such
      requests must be forwarded to the DPO within 24 hours; we have 30
      days to respond (extendable to 60 days for complex requests).
  (e) DATA BREACH NOTIFICATION: Any suspected or actual personal data
      breach (unauthorised access, use, disclosure, copying, modification,
      disposal, or loss of personal data) must be reported IMMEDIATELY to
      the DPO. Notifiable data breaches (those likely to result in
      significant harm or affect more than 500 individuals) must be
      notified to PDPC within 3 calendar days.
  (f) DATA RETENTION AND DISPOSAL: Do not retain personal data longer than
      necessary for the purpose for which it was collected. Workday and
      Salesforce are configured with automatic retention rules per the
      Records Retention Schedule. Do not download and save personal data
      to local drives, USB drives, or personal cloud storage.

APPENDIX B: NOTICE PERIODS & SEVERANCE
================================================================================

During Probation:
  - Either party may terminate the employment by giving FOURTEEN (14)
    CALENDAR DAYS' written notice, or salary in lieu thereof.
  - Applies uniformly across all grades.

Post-Probation (Confirmed Employees):

| GRADE RANGE       | NOTICE PERIOD BY EMPLOYEE          | NOTICE PERIOD BY EMPLOYER          |
|-------------------|-------------------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| Grades A, B, C    | 30 Calendar Days (1 calendar month) | 30 Calendar Days (1 calendar month) |
| Grades C+, D, E,  | 60 Calendar Days (2 calendar months)| 60 Calendar Days (2 calendar months)|
|        E+         |                                     |                                     |

Additional provisions:
  - Notice given by the employer may be paid in lieu (PILON) at any time,
    with or without cause. When PILON is paid, the employee's last working
    day is the date of the letter; the employee does not need to attend
    work and access is revoked immediately.
  - Notice given by an employee must be in writing via Resignation Letter
    submitted in Workday. Employees who fail to give the required notice
    will have the shortfall deducted from final salary.

Severance Pay on Redundancy:
Where employment is terminated by reason of redundancy (not performance,
not misconduct), an employee who has completed at least 2 years of
continuous service is entitled to severance pay in addition to the
required notice period, at the following rates:
  - 2 to >3 years service:         1 week per year of service
  - 3 to >5 years service:         1.5 weeks per year of service
  - 5 to >10 years service:        2 weeks per year of service
  - 10+ years service:             2.5 weeks per year of service,
                                   capped at 25 weeks of base salary

APPENDIX C: FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS (FAQ)
================================================================================

C1. When should I expect my salary?
    Salaries are disbursed on the 25th of each month, or the last business
    day before if the 25th falls on a weekend or public holiday.

C2. How do I change my bank account or personal details?
    Submit Form PAY-04 (bank account) or Form PR-01 (personal details) via
    Workday. Allow 10 working days before the next payroll for changes.

C3. Can I borrow against my future leave or get a salary advance?
    Salary advances of up to 50% of one month's base salary are available
    once per calendar year for genuine emergencies (medical, bereavement,
    natural disaster). Repayment is deducted equally over the following
    3 months' payroll. Submit Form SADV-01 to Payroll.

C4. What is the company's stance on office romances?
    Consensual romantic or sexual relationships between employees are
    permitted but must be disclosed to People Operations via Form REL-01
    if either party reports to the other, or if one holds a position of
    authority over the other (e.g. on the same hiring / promotion panel).
    Undisclosed relationships in a reporting line are a disciplinary
    matter.

C5. Can I work for DBizLabs from another country for personal reasons?
    Temporary remote work of up to 30 calendar days per calendar year from
    an overseas jurisdiction is permitted with prior HRBP and Finance
    approval (permanent tax and immigration implications are assessed on
    a case basis). Longer periods require CFO approval.

C6. Who owns the data and code I produce during Hackathon / Innovation Week?
    All work produced during DBizLabs-sponsored events (hackathons,
    innovation days, etc.) — whether during or outside work hours — is
    the property of DBizLabs Pte. Ltd., regardless of whether it is
    subsequently commercialised. Winning teams receive prizes but no
    separate IP ownership.

C7. What happens to my ESOPs if the company is acquired?
    In the event of a "change of control" (acquisition, merger, sale of
    substantially all assets), the Board and RemCo will determine, per
    the ESOP Plan Rules, whether to accelerate vesting (commonly "single
    trigger": 12 months acceleration, or "double trigger": acceleration
    upon both change of control AND involuntary termination within
    12 months). The default position is double-trigger acceleration.

C8. How do I report a concern that I don't feel comfortable taking to my
    manager or HRBP?
    Use the independent Whistleblower Channel described in Section 7.7.
    The channel is confidential, anonymous if preferred, and reports
    directly to the Chairman of the Board / Audit & Risk Committee.

C9. Can I use my DBizLabs email address for personal purposes?
    Occasional personal use (e.g. sending a family photo, booking a
    personal appointment) is acceptable so long as it does not: use
    significant resources, create liability for the company, interfere
    with job duties, or involve illegal activity. Do NOT use your
    DBizLabs email on social media sign-ups, forums, or political
    campaigns.

C10. What is the pet policy for the Marina One office?
     Dogs (small to medium breeds, fully vaccinated, house-trained,
     leashed at all times) are permitted in the Level 22 office on
     "Bring Your Dog to Work Day" — the last Friday of each month. A
     maximum of 4 dogs per day; handlers must pre-register via Form
     PET-01 with Facilities. Cats and other pets are not allowed. No
     animals at any time in the server rooms, kitchen, or quiet rooms.
